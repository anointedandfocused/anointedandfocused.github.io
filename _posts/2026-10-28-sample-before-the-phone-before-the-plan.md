For a long time, the first thing I touched each day was a glass screen. Headlines, messages, and notifications all got my attention before I had said a single word to the God who gave me the day.

Nothing about that felt dramatic. It just quietly set the order of my life: the world first, then God, if there was time left over.

## The order matters

Jesus lived the opposite way. Mark records one ordinary morning in His ministry, when the crowds were already looking for Him:

> "And rising very early in the morning, while it was still dark, he departed and went out to a desolate place, and there he prayed."
>
> <cite>Mark 1:35 (ESV)</cite>

Notice what came first. Before the demands of the day, He went to the Father. If the Son of God needed that, I certainly do.

## What I'm practicing

Nothing about this is complicated, and none of it is a formula. It is simply how I'm trying to put God first at the start of the day:

1. **The phone stays in another room.** I do not pick it up until I have prayed.
2. **I read one passage slowly.** One passage, read more than once, beats a chapter skimmed.
3. **I write down one sentence.** Just what stood out, in my own words.
4. **I pray over the day ahead.** My work, my family, and the people I will meet.

### Some mornings are messy

There are mornings when I oversleep, my mind wanders, or I get it all wrong. I'm learning that the goal is not a perfect quiet time. The goal is to come to Christ first, again, however the morning goes.

## Seek first

This is what Jesus told His followers about priorities:

> "But seek first the kingdom of God and His righteousness, and all these things will be added to you."
>
> <cite>Matthew 6:33</cite>

I used to read that verse as a promise about getting what I want. Now I read it as an invitation to start with Him and trust Him with the rest.

If you've been starting your day with a screen, I'm not writing to shame you. I've been there. I'm only inviting you to try one small change tomorrow morning: *Christ first, then everything else.*
