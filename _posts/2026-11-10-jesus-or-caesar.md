## Main Message Summary

Continuing the upside-down kingdom of Matthew 18, Jesus issues one of His strongest warnings: it would be better for a person to be drowned than to cause one of His "little ones" to stumble (Matthew 18:6-9). The "little ones" aren't only children. They are the *mikros*, the people of low status at the bottom of the pyramid, and Jesus is speaking to the *megas*, those with power and influence. God's promise of consequences for those who abuse or use others for selfish gain is itself an act of love. Everyone will eventually have influence over someone, and the question for each of us is simple: when the people under our influence look at how we use it, do they see Jesus or Caesar?

## The Devotional

When most of us picture heaven, we think of streets of gold and mansions. But listen to what Jesus actually talks about, and the picture shifts. Continuing from last week's upside-down kingdom, Jesus keeps lifting up the ordinary, the overlooked, and the least. In His kingdom, the regular people are not the afterthought. They are the ones He is protecting.

That protective love is why His words in Matthew 18:6 are so severe: <span class="jesus">"If anyone causes one of these little ones, those who believe in me, to stumble, it would be better for them to have a large millstone hung around their neck and to be drowned in the depths of the sea."</span> He goes on to say that if your hand or foot or eye causes you to stumble, cut it off, gouge it out, and throw it away (18:8-9).

Some of us feel anger just reading that, or anger at injustice in the world. That's a human reaction, and it isn't always wrong. We're made in God's image, and God feels it too. He finds abuse wrong. "Be angry and do not sin," Paul says (Ephesians 4:26). When God promises punishment for those who abuse others, that isn't cruelty. It's love, because He is standing up for the ones who can't stand up for themselves.

So who are the "little ones"? Not necessarily children. The Greek word for "little" here is *mikros*, which describes low or unimportant status, the least, the bottom of the pyramid, last in line. Its opposite is *megas*: high status, important, top of the pyramid, first in line. This connects directly to last week, when Jesus turned the pyramid upside down. Now He is warning the *megas* about how they treat the *mikros*. The sin He names is abuse: using people for your own selfish gain.

The "cut it off" language is deliberate, vivid exaggeration, not a call to hurt ourselves. Jesus is showing how seriously we should treat anything in our lives that we use for our own advantage at the expense of others. Cut it out now.

The alternative is simple, and Jesus has already modeled it: the *megas* serve the *mikros*. So how are you using the power or influence God has entrusted to you? Do the people under you see Jesus or Caesar? Caesar used power to be served. Jesus used His to serve and give His life for others (Matthew 20:25-28).

And if you think you have no influence, think again. You may be a parent shaping your children. You may be a student influencing your friends and the wider student body. You may be a worker influencing a team. Jesus told the world to go and make disciples (Matthew 28:19), which assumes we all have influence on someone.

Paul puts it directly in his letter to Corinth, writing about the strong and the weak: "Be careful, however, that the exercise of your rights does not become a stumbling block to the weak... When you sin against them in this way and wound their weak conscience, you sin against Christ" (1 Corinthians 8:9, 12). If our sin leads someone weaker to sin, it's no small thing.

So ask yourself: what am I doing that could cause someone to stumble? We will all eventually be *megas* speaking into the life of a *mikros* somewhere. You have a choice. Will you use what you've been given to get what you want, or to express Jesus? Pray that the gifts He has entrusted to you never become a reason someone else stumbles, and ask Him to help you push back your natural tendencies.

## Biblical Examples

**Jesus Clearing the Temple (Mark 11:15-17).** Seeing the vulnerable exploited in the place meant for prayer, Jesus acted with righteous anger. It's a picture of the right kind of anger, one that rises up for the exploited rather than for ourselves.

**David and Uriah (2 Samuel 11-12).** As king, David used his power to take what wasn't his and arrange the death of a loyal man. Nathan confronted him, and David's later repentance (Psalm 51) shows the weight of power misused. It's a sobering example of a *megas* using his position for himself.

**Boaz and Ruth (Ruth 2).** Boaz, a wealthy and respected landowner, used his influence to protect and provide for a poor foreign widow gleaning in his field. He is a clear picture of the *megas* serving the *mikros*.

## Practical Applications

1. **Take an influence inventory.** Write down the people over whom you have influence, such as children, coworkers, friends, or students, and honestly note how you tend to use that influence.
2. **Ask "Jesus or Caesar?"** Before an important interaction this week, ask yourself whether you are using your position to get something for yourself or to serve the person in front of you.
3. **Cut something out.** Identify one habit, relationship, or practice that you use for your own advantage or that could cause someone else to stumble, and take a concrete step to remove it now.
4. **Serve someone "under" you.** Do something practical for a person who looks to you, such as a child, an employee, or a younger believer, with nothing expected in return.
5. **Pray for righteous anger.** Choose one injustice you notice this week and ask God how He wants you to respond in service or advocacy, rather than only reacting in anger.

## Reflection Questions

1. When the people under my influence look at how I use it, do they see Jesus or Caesar?
2. Where do I have power or influence that I haven't acknowledged?
3. Is there anything I do that could cause someone weaker in faith or position to stumble?
4. What am I holding on to that I need to cut out now because it serves my advantage at someone else's expense?
5. Who are the "little ones" in my life, and how can I serve them this week?

## Closing Prayer

Lord, thank You that You love the least, the overlooked, and the ordinary. Thank You for standing up for those who can't stand up for themselves. Forgive me for the times I've used my influence for my own advantage, and for any way I've caused someone to stumble. Show me the people You've placed under my care, and help me use the gifts You've given me to express Jesus rather than to get what I want. Give me the courage to cut out whatever hinders that. Help me push back my natural tendencies, and make me a servant to the people You've entrusted to me. In Jesus' name, Amen.

## Key Takeaway

**Influence is a gift entrusted to serve the least, so let the people under your care see Jesus, not Caesar.**
