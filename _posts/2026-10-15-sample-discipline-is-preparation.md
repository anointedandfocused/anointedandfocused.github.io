I used to think of discipline as a punishment, something I owed because I had failed to do better. Early alarms, strict routines, and saying no to things I wanted all felt like penalties.

Then I started noticing how Scripture talks about it. Discipline isn't God's way of making life smaller. It's how He prepares us for the life He has in mind.

## Train for godliness

Paul uses the language of a trainer:

> "Train yourself for godliness; for while bodily training is of some value, godliness is of value in every way, as it holds promise for the present life and also for the life to come."
>
> <cite>1 Timothy 4:7-8 (ESV)</cite>

Training implies a goal. An athlete does not train out of guilt. They train because they are preparing for something. The same is true for my prayer life, my habits, and my character.

## Small and faithful

It is tempting to think discipline means dramatic change. In my experience, it looks more like quiet repetition.

> "One who is faithful in a very little is also faithful in much."
>
> <cite>Luke 16:10 (ESV)</cite>

Faithfulness in small things is the training ground. The habits nobody sees are the ones that shape who I am when the pressure comes.

### What this looks like in a week

- **Show up on time** for the things I said I would do.
- **Keep my word**, even in small commitments.
- **Take care of my body**, because it is part of what God entrusted to me.
- **Protect time for Scripture and prayer** before it gets crowded out.

## When I'm tired

There will be weeks when I miss the habit, lose momentum, or want to quit. Paul's encouragement to the Galatians is one I keep close:

> "And let us not grow weary of doing good, for in due season we will reap, if we do not give up."
>
> <cite>Galatians 6:9 (ESV)</cite>

I can't make myself holy by willpower. But I can say yes to the daily steps and trust God to do what only He can do.

**Discipline isn't punishment. It's preparation.** What is one small area where God might be preparing you right now?
